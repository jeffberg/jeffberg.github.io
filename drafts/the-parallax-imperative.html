A Note Before the Dive

This paper is the first public artifact of the PARALLAX™ research program. It makes a simple claim with deep consequences: any single view of a complex system is mathematically guaranteed to distort. This is not a metaphor. It is a theorem — Gauss, 1827 — and its implications for how we analyze data remain fundamental and underexplored.

What follows are seven structured queries on public oceanographic data. Each query looks at the same ocean through a different analytical lens. Each reveals structure that the others cannot see. Together, they demonstrate why the field’s fifty-year debate about which dimension reduction technique is “best” has been asking the wrong question.

The ocean was chosen for its legibility, not its novelty. The framework was engineered for systems far less transparent than water.

Lens I — Temporal-Developmental

How does the thermal memory of the deep ocean differ from its surface across decades — and does the signal of warming exceed the noise of natural variability?

WITH decade_memory AS ( SELECT EXTRACT(DECADE FROM observation_ts) * 10 AS decade, CASE WHEN pressure_dbar < 100 THEN 'surface' WHEN pressure_dbar < 1000 THEN 'mesopelagic' ELSE 'abyssal' END AS depth_realm, AVG(temperature_c) AS mean_temp_c, STDDEV(temperature_c) AS temp_variability, COUNT(*) AS n_observations FROM argo.float_profiles WHERE quality_flag IN (1, 2) GROUP BY decade, depth_realm ), warming_signal AS ( SELECT depth_realm, decade, mean_temp_c - LAG(mean_temp_c) OVER ( PARTITION BY depth_realm ORDER BY decade ) AS decadal_shift_c, temp_variability FROM decade_memory ) SELECT depth_realm, decade, ROUND(decadal_shift_c::numeric, 4) AS warming_per_decade, ROUND(temp_variability::numeric, 4) AS variability, CASE WHEN ABS(decadal_shift_c) > temp_variability THEN 'signal exceeds noise' ELSE 'within natural range' END AS interpretation FROM warming_signal WHERE decadal_shift_c IS NOT NULL ORDER BY depth_realm, decade;
The surface warms loudly, visibly, politically. But this query asks whether the deep ocean remembers differently. The abyssal signal is quieter, slower, and — measured against its own variability — often larger. What the surface announces, the deep has already absorbed. A single depth slice would tell only one story.

Lens II — Psychometric

When a buoy and a float are lowered into the same water at the same hour, do they agree about what they measure — and what does their disagreement reveal?

WITH co_located_readings AS ( SELECT b.station_id, f.float_id, b.observation_ts, b.sea_surface_temp_c AS buoy_sst, f.temperature_c AS float_sst, SQRT( POWER(b.lat - f.lat, 2) + POWER(b.lon - f.lon, 2) ) AS spatial_gap_deg FROM noaa.buoy_observations b JOIN argo.float_profiles f ON ABS(b.lat - f.lat) < 0.5 AND ABS(b.lon - f.lon) < 0.5 AND ABS(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM b.observation_ts - f.observation_ts)) < 3600 WHERE f.pressure_dbar < 10 AND f.quality_flag = 1 ), agreement AS ( SELECT COUNT(*) AS n_pairs, CORR(buoy_sst, float_sst) AS pearson_r, AVG(buoy_sst - float_sst) AS mean_bias, STDDEV(buoy_sst - float_sst) AS disagreement, AVG(spatial_gap_deg) AS avg_gap_deg FROM co_located_readings ) SELECT n_pairs, ROUND(pearson_r::numeric, 4) AS reliability, ROUND(mean_bias::numeric, 4) AS systematic_bias, ROUND(disagreement::numeric, 4) AS measurement_noise, CASE WHEN pearson_r > 0.95 THEN 'instruments converge' WHEN pearson_r > 0.80 THEN 'moderate agreement' ELSE 'instruments diverge' END AS verdict FROM agreement;
Psychometrics asks the question that most physical scientists skip: do our instruments agree with each other? Reliability is not assumed — it is tested. The disagreement between co-located sensors is not error. It is information: evidence that what we call “sea surface temperature” is not a single thing but a construct whose measurement properties depend on who is asking, from where, with what instrument.

Lens III — Network-Theoretic

Which ocean currents connect the world’s basins into a single circulatory system — and which regions are islands, unreached by the global flow?

WITH flow_grid AS ( SELECT ROUND(lat / 5) * 5 AS lat_bin, ROUND(lon / 5) * 5 AS lon_bin, AVG(velocity_east_ms) AS mean_u, AVG(velocity_north_ms) AS mean_v, AVG(speed_ms) AS mean_speed, COUNT(*) AS n_obs FROM ocean.current_measurements WHERE depth_m < 200 GROUP BY lat_bin, lon_bin HAVING COUNT(*) >= 10 ), edges AS ( SELECT a.lat_bin AS from_lat, a.lon_bin AS from_lon, b.lat_bin AS to_lat, b.lon_bin AS to_lon, a.mean_speed AS edge_weight FROM flow_grid a JOIN flow_grid b ON b.lat_bin = a.lat_bin + ROUND(a.mean_v * 86400 / 111000 / 5) * 5 AND b.lon_bin = a.lon_bin + ROUND(a.mean_u * 86400 / (111000 * COS(RADIANS(a.lat_bin))) / 5) * 5 WHERE a.mean_speed > 0.05 ) SELECT from_lat, from_lon, to_lat, to_lon, ROUND(edge_weight::numeric, 3) AS current_strength, COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY from_lat, from_lon) AS out_degree, COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY to_lat, to_lon) AS in_degree FROM edges ORDER BY edge_weight DESC LIMIT 50;
The ocean is not a volume. It is a graph. Currents create edges; water masses create nodes. The Gulf Stream is not merely warm water moving north — it is the highest-bandwidth connection in the Atlantic’s network topology. The Southern Ocean is not merely cold — it is the most connected hub on the planet. This query builds the adjacency structure that no single time-series or temperature profile could reveal.

Lens IV — Causal-Inferential

Can we separate the signal of warming from the noise of natural variability — and at which latitudes does the effect size cross from negligible to undeniable?

WITH baseline_era AS ( SELECT lat, lon, AVG(sst_c) AS baseline_sst, STDDEV(sst_c) AS baseline_var FROM noaa.sea_surface_temperature WHERE observation_date BETWEEN '1985-01-01' AND '1995-12-31' GROUP BY lat, lon HAVING COUNT(*) >= 100 ), modern_era AS ( SELECT lat, lon, AVG(sst_c) AS modern_sst, AVG(sst_anomaly_c) AS mean_anomaly FROM noaa.sea_surface_temperature WHERE observation_date >= '2015-01-01' GROUP BY lat, lon HAVING COUNT(*) >= 100 ), quasi_experiment AS ( SELECT b.lat, b.lon, m.modern_sst - b.baseline_sst AS observed_shift, b.baseline_var, (m.modern_sst - b.baseline_sst) / NULLIF(b.baseline_var, 0) AS effect_size, m.mean_anomaly FROM baseline_era b JOIN modern_era m USING (lat, lon) ) SELECT ROUND(lat) AS latitude_band, ROUND(AVG(observed_shift)::numeric, 3) AS mean_shift_c, ROUND(AVG(effect_size)::numeric, 3) AS cohens_d, ROUND(AVG(mean_anomaly)::numeric, 3) AS mean_anomaly, CASE WHEN AVG(effect_size) > 0.8 THEN 'large effect' WHEN AVG(effect_size) > 0.5 THEN 'medium effect' WHEN AVG(effect_size) > 0.2 THEN 'small effect' ELSE 'negligible' END AS magnitude FROM quasi_experiment GROUP BY ROUND(lat) ORDER BY latitude_band;
Econometrics brings the discipline of causal thinking to physical data. The “quasi-experiment” here treats baseline-era and modern-era sea surface temperatures as pre- and post-treatment observations, computing Cohen’s d — the standardized effect size — at each latitude band. The question is not “is the ocean warmer?” (it is) but “at which latitudes does the shift exceed the system’s own natural vocabulary?” The causal lens does not accept a trend line. It demands an effect size.

Lens V — Dynamical Systems

Is the ocean’s thermal behavior approaching a tipping point — or cycling within its historical dynamic range? Does the system remember itself from year to year?

WITH monthly_state AS ( SELECT DATE_TRUNC('month', observation_date) AS month, AVG(sst_c) AS global_sst, STDDEV(sst_c) AS spatial_variance FROM noaa.sea_surface_temperature WHERE lat BETWEEN -60 AND 60 GROUP BY DATE_TRUNC('month', observation_date) ), lagged_state AS ( SELECT month, global_sst, spatial_variance, LAG(global_sst, 1) OVER (ORDER BY month) AS sst_lag1, LAG(global_sst, 12) OVER (ORDER BY month) AS sst_lag12 FROM monthly_state ), sensitivity AS ( SELECT EXTRACT(YEAR FROM month) AS year, CORR(global_sst, sst_lag1) AS persistence_1m, CORR(global_sst, sst_lag12) AS memory_12m, AVG(spatial_variance) AS mean_spatial_var, REGR_SLOPE( global_sst, EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM month) ) AS trend_slope FROM lagged_state WHERE sst_lag1 IS NOT NULL AND sst_lag12 IS NOT NULL GROUP BY EXTRACT(YEAR FROM month) ) SELECT year, ROUND(persistence_1m::numeric, 4) AS autocorrelation_1m, ROUND(memory_12m::numeric, 4) AS annual_memory, ROUND(mean_spatial_var::numeric, 4) AS spatial_variance, CASE WHEN persistence_1m > 0.95 AND mean_spatial_var > LAG(mean_spatial_var) OVER (ORDER BY year) THEN 'critical slowing detected' ELSE 'within dynamic range' END AS stability_signal FROM sensitivity ORDER BY year;
Dynamical systems theory offers a vocabulary that neither time-series statistics nor causal inference possesses: the concept of the tipping point. When a system approaches a critical transition, two signatures emerge simultaneously — rising autocorrelation (the system takes longer to forget a perturbation) and rising variance (the system’s fluctuations widen). This query computes both, year by year. The temporal lens sees warming. The dynamical lens asks whether the warming is approaching a threshold from which return becomes nonlinear.

Lens VI — Geopolitical

Whose ocean gets studied? Which nations deploy the instruments, and which ocean zones does their gaze reach — or fail to reach?

WITH deployment_census AS ( SELECT country, COUNT(DISTINCT float_id) AS floats_deployed, AVG(deploy_lat) AS mean_lat, AVG(deploy_lon) AS mean_lon, MIN(deploy_date) AS first_deployment, MAX(deploy_date) AS latest_deployment FROM argo.float_metadata WHERE status = 'ACTIVE' GROUP BY country ), observation_equity AS ( SELECT CASE WHEN f.lat BETWEEN -60 AND -30 THEN 'southern ocean' WHEN f.lat BETWEEN -30 AND 0 THEN 'tropical south' WHEN f.lat BETWEEN 0 AND 30 THEN 'tropical north' WHEN f.lat BETWEEN 30 AND 60 THEN 'temperate north' ELSE 'polar' END AS ocean_zone, m.country, COUNT(*) AS n_profiles, COUNT(DISTINCT m.float_id) AS n_floats FROM argo.float_profiles f JOIN argo.float_metadata m USING (float_id) WHERE f.quality_flag IN (1, 2) GROUP BY ocean_zone, m.country ) SELECT ocean_zone, country, n_floats, n_profiles, ROUND( 100.0 * n_profiles / SUM(n_profiles) OVER (PARTITION BY ocean_zone), 1 ) AS pct_of_zone, RANK() OVER ( PARTITION BY ocean_zone ORDER BY n_profiles DESC ) AS observation_rank FROM observation_equity ORDER BY ocean_zone, observation_rank LIMIT 30;
The geopolitical lens reveals what no physical measurement can: the social structure of observation itself. The Argo program is international in principle but deeply asymmetric in practice. A handful of nations deploy the vast majority of floats; vast regions of the Southern Hemisphere are measured by instruments deployed thousands of kilometers away by countries that border different oceans. The data are not biased in the statistical sense. They are biased in the political sense — and the two are inseparable.

Lens VII — Multi-Scale

Does the pattern that holds at one degree of latitude hold at ten? When we zoom out, does the structure cohere — or does it fracture into something the fine grain could not predict?

WITH fine_grain AS ( SELECT ROUND(lat) AS lat_1deg, ROUND(lon) AS lon_1deg, AVG(sst_anomaly_c) AS anomaly_fine, COUNT(*) AS n_obs FROM noaa.sea_surface_temperature WHERE observation_date >= '2020-01-01' GROUP BY ROUND(lat), ROUND(lon) HAVING COUNT(*) >= 30 ), coarse_grain AS ( SELECT ROUND(lat / 10) * 10 AS lat_10deg, ROUND(lon / 10) * 10 AS lon_10deg, AVG(sst_anomaly_c) AS anomaly_coarse, STDDEV(sst_anomaly_c) AS within_cell_var, COUNT(*) AS n_obs FROM noaa.sea_surface_temperature WHERE observation_date >= '2020-01-01' GROUP BY ROUND(lat / 10) * 10, ROUND(lon / 10) * 10 HAVING COUNT(*) >= 100 ), scale_comparison AS ( SELECT c.lat_10deg, c.lon_10deg, c.anomaly_coarse, c.within_cell_var, AVG(f.anomaly_fine) AS mean_fine_in_cell, STDDEV(f.anomaly_fine) AS fine_heterogeneity, COUNT(f.*) AS n_fine_cells FROM coarse_grain c JOIN fine_grain f ON f.lat_1deg BETWEEN c.lat_10deg AND c.lat_10deg + 9 AND f.lon_1deg BETWEEN c.lon_10deg AND c.lon_10deg + 9 GROUP BY c.lat_10deg, c.lon_10deg, c.anomaly_coarse, c.within_cell_var ) SELECT lat_10deg, lon_10deg, ROUND(anomaly_coarse::numeric, 3) AS coarse_signal, ROUND(fine_heterogeneity::numeric, 3) AS fine_texture, ROUND( (fine_heterogeneity / NULLIF(ABS(anomaly_coarse), 0))::numeric, 2 ) AS scale_ratio, CASE WHEN fine_heterogeneity < ABS(anomaly_coarse) * 0.3 THEN 'coherent across scales' WHEN fine_heterogeneity > ABS(anomaly_coarse) THEN 'scale-dependent structure' ELSE 'mixed coherence' END AS scale_verdict FROM scale_comparison WHERE n_fine_cells >= 5 ORDER BY scale_ratio DESC LIMIT 25;
The multi-scale lens asks the most unsettling question in data science: does your result depend on your resolution? This query compares SST anomaly structure at 1° and 10° grids. Where the coarse signal is smooth but the fine texture is turbulent, the pattern is scale-dependent — a finding invisible to any analysis conducted at a single resolution. The implication: conclusions drawn at one zoom level may not survive a change of aperture. Scale is not a parameter. It is a lens.

Coda

Seven lenses. One ocean. Each query legal, public, reproducible. Each revealing structure invisible to the others.

The thesis of PARALLAX™ is not that multiple views are nice to have. It is that single views are mathematically guaranteed to distort — and that the distortions are invisible to the view that created them. This is not an opinion. It is the content of Gauss’s Theorema Egregium (1827), applied to the manifold learning problem that underlies modern data science.

The temporal lens sees warming. The psychometric lens asks whether the instruments agree about what they are measuring. The network lens reveals connectivity invisible to any thermometer. The causal lens demands effect sizes, not trend lines. The dynamical lens tests for tipping points. The geopolitical lens exposes whose gaze shapes the data. The multi-scale lens asks whether the pattern survives a change of resolution.

No single lens sees all of this. No single lens can.

We demonstrated this on seawater because seawater is patient, public, and well-instrumented. Every query above could be re-pointed — at behavioral data, at platform telemetry, at neural recordings, at market dynamics, at cultural corpora — without changing a single structural element. The SQL would change. The schema would change. The parallax would not.

The framework was engineered for systems far less transparent than water.

There is no single view. There is only the ensemble.

— Jeffrey J. Berg
