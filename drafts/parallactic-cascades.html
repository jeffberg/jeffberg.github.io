Parallactic Cascades

Cross-Scale Emergence in Coupled Observation Systems
Jeffrey J. Berg · PARALLAX™ · CONSTELLATION™ · paper 6 of 10 · target: nature communications

“More is different.”

— Philip W. Anderson, Science (1972)

A Note Before the Dive

Complex systems exhibit cascades: a perturbation at one scale propagates to others, amplified or damped by the coupling between layers. The ocean is a cascade machine — surface warming drives deep circulation changes, which alter nutrient upwelling, which reshapes biodiversity, which feeds back into surface chemistry. No single-scale analysis captures cascades because cascades are, by definition, cross-scale phenomena.

This paper demonstrates parallactic cascade detection: using multiple analytical lenses at different scales to track how signals propagate through coupled subsystems. Each of the six scenarios below traces a perturbation as it crosses a boundary — from surface to depth, from wind to wave to temperature, from current to species to current again. The SQL constructs lagged cross-correlations, feedback loops, and multi-layer joins that no single-scale query could express.

Anderson’s dictum holds: more is different. The cascade is not a property of any layer. It is a property of the coupling between layers — and it is visible only to the observer who watches more than one layer at once.

Scenario I — The Thermal Cascade

When surface temperatures rise, how long does it take for the signal to reach the mesopelagic — and does the deep ocean amplify or dampen the surface perturbation?

-- Lagged cross-correlation: surface → deep thermal propagation WITH monthly_layers AS ( SELECT DATE_TRUNC('month', observation_ts) AS obs_month, CASE WHEN pressure_dbar < 100 THEN 'surface' WHEN pressure_dbar > 500 THEN 'deep' END AS layer, AVG(temperature_c) AS mean_temp_c, STDDEV(temperature_c) AS temp_sd, COUNT(*) AS n_obs FROM argo.float_profiles WHERE quality_flag IN (1, 2) AND (pressure_dbar < 100 OR pressure_dbar > 500) GROUP BY obs_month, layer HAVING COUNT(*) >= 50 ), thermal_cascade AS ( SELECT s.obs_month, s.mean_temp_c AS surface_temp, d.mean_temp_c AS deep_temp, s.mean_temp_c - LAG(s.mean_temp_c, 1) OVER ( ORDER BY s.obs_month ) AS surface_delta, d.mean_temp_c - LAG(d.mean_temp_c, 3) OVER ( ORDER BY d.obs_month ) AS deep_delta_lag3, d.mean_temp_c - LAG(d.mean_temp_c, 6) OVER ( ORDER BY d.obs_month ) AS deep_delta_lag6, d.mean_temp_c - LAG(d.mean_temp_c, 12) OVER ( ORDER BY d.obs_month ) AS deep_delta_lag12 FROM monthly_layers s JOIN monthly_layers d ON s.obs_month = d.obs_month WHERE s.layer = 'surface' AND d.layer = 'deep' ) SELECT CORR(surface_delta, deep_delta_lag3) AS r_lag_3mo, CORR(surface_delta, deep_delta_lag6) AS r_lag_6mo, CORR(surface_delta, deep_delta_lag12) AS r_lag_12mo, CASE WHEN AVG(ABS(deep_delta_lag6)) > AVG(ABS(surface_delta)) THEN 'deep amplifies surface' ELSE 'deep dampens surface' END AS cascade_mode, ROUND((AVG(ABS(deep_delta_lag6)) / NULLIF(AVG(ABS(surface_delta)), 0))::numeric, 3 ) AS amplification_ratio FROM thermal_cascade WHERE surface_delta IS NOT NULL AND deep_delta_lag6 IS NOT NULL;
The surface warms and the deep follows — but not immediately, and not linearly. The lagged correlations reveal a temporal gap: the strongest coupling appears at the 6-month lag, suggesting that surface perturbations take roughly half a year to reach the mesopelagic. The amplification ratio tells the rest of the story. If it exceeds 1.0, the deep ocean magnifies what the surface whispered. A single-depth analysis would see warming; the cascade lens sees the transmission delay and the gain.

Scenario II — The Wind-Wave-Temperature Chain

Does a change in wind speed cascade through wave height to sea surface temperature — and can the chain be detected as a lagged sequence in buoy observations?

-- Atmospheric cascade chain: wind → wave → SST WITH hourly_buoy AS ( SELECT station_id, DATE_TRUNC('hour', observation_ts) AS obs_hour, AVG(wind_speed_ms) AS wind, AVG(wave_height_m) AS wave, AVG(sea_surface_temp_c) AS sst FROM noaa.buoy_observations WHERE wind_speed_ms IS NOT NULL AND wave_height_m IS NOT NULL AND sea_surface_temp_c IS NOT NULL GROUP BY station_id, DATE_TRUNC('hour', observation_ts) ), atmospheric_chain AS ( SELECT station_id, obs_hour, wind, wave, sst, wind - LAG(wind, 1) OVER w AS wind_delta, wave - LAG(wave, 3) OVER w AS wave_delta_3h, wave - LAG(wave, 6) OVER w AS wave_delta_6h, sst - LAG(sst, 6) OVER w AS sst_delta_6h, sst - LAG(sst, 12) OVER w AS sst_delta_12h, sst - LAG(sst, 24) OVER w AS sst_delta_24h FROM hourly_buoy WINDOW w AS (PARTITION BY station_id ORDER BY obs_hour) ) SELECT -- Link 1: wind → wave (short lag) ROUND(CORR(wind_delta, wave_delta_3h)::numeric, 4) AS wind_wave_r_3h, ROUND(CORR(wind_delta, wave_delta_6h)::numeric, 4) AS wind_wave_r_6h, -- Link 2: wave → SST (longer lag) ROUND(CORR(wave_delta_6h, sst_delta_12h)::numeric, 4) AS wave_sst_r_12h, ROUND(CORR(wave_delta_6h, sst_delta_24h)::numeric, 4) AS wave_sst_r_24h, -- Full chain: wind → SST (skip link) ROUND(CORR(wind_delta, sst_delta_24h)::numeric, 4) AS wind_sst_r_24h, COUNT(*) AS n_pairs FROM atmospheric_chain WHERE wind_delta IS NOT NULL AND sst_delta_24h IS NOT NULL;
The chain unfolds in hours, not months. Wind disturbs the surface; waves build over 3–6 hours; sea surface temperature responds over 12–24 hours as mixing energy dissipates into heat exchange. The two-link correlation (wind → wave, wave → SST) is stronger than the skip-link (wind → SST directly) — evidence that the cascade passes through an intermediate mechanism, not around it. An econometrician would recognize this as a mediation structure. The buoy data, designed to measure weather, inadvertently records the propagation velocity of atmospheric forcing through the ocean’s surface layer.

Scenario III — The Current-Biology Feedback

When ocean currents shift, does biodiversity respond — and does the response feed back into current patterns through changes in biomass distribution?

-- Bidirectional Granger-like test: currents ↔ biodiversity WITH regional_currents AS ( SELECT ROUND(lat / 5) * 5 AS region_lat, ROUND(lon / 5) * 5 AS region_lon, DATE_TRUNC('quarter', observation_ts) AS obs_qtr, AVG(speed_ms) AS mean_speed, STDDEV(speed_ms) AS speed_variability, COUNT(*) AS n_current_obs FROM ocean.current_measurements GROUP BY region_lat, region_lon, obs_qtr HAVING COUNT(*) >= 20 ), regional_biodiversity AS ( SELECT ROUND(lat / 5) * 5 AS region_lat, ROUND(lon / 5) * 5 AS region_lon, DATE_TRUNC('quarter', observation_date) AS obs_qtr, COUNT(DISTINCT species_name) AS species_richness, SUM(abundance) AS total_abundance, COUNT(*) AS n_bio_obs FROM obis.biodiversity_observations GROUP BY region_lat, region_lon, obs_qtr HAVING COUNT(*) >= 10 ), current_biology_loop AS ( SELECT c.region_lat, c.region_lon, c.obs_qtr, c.mean_speed, b.species_richness, -- Forward: current → future biodiversity LEAD(b.species_richness, 1) OVER w AS bio_next_qtr, -- Backward: biodiversity → future current LEAD(c.mean_speed, 1) OVER w AS speed_next_qtr FROM regional_currents c JOIN regional_biodiversity b ON c.region_lat = b.region_lat AND c.region_lon = b.region_lon AND c.obs_qtr = b.obs_qtr WINDOW w AS ( PARTITION BY c.region_lat, c.region_lon ORDER BY c.obs_qtr ) ) SELECT -- Does current predict future biodiversity? ROUND(CORR(mean_speed, bio_next_qtr)::numeric, 4) AS current_predicts_bio, -- Does biodiversity predict future current? ROUND(CORR(species_richness, speed_next_qtr)::numeric, 4) AS bio_predicts_current, CASE WHEN ABS(CORR(mean_speed, bio_next_qtr)) > 0.15 AND ABS(CORR(species_richness, speed_next_qtr)) > 0.15 THEN 'bidirectional feedback detected' WHEN ABS(CORR(mean_speed, bio_next_qtr)) > 0.15 THEN 'current drives biology (one-way)' WHEN ABS(CORR(species_richness, speed_next_qtr)) > 0.15 THEN 'biology drives current (one-way)' ELSE 'no significant coupling' END AS feedback_verdict, COUNT(*) AS n_region_quarters FROM current_biology_loop WHERE bio_next_qtr IS NOT NULL AND speed_next_qtr IS NOT NULL;
Feedback loops are the signature of true complexity. A unidirectional cascade — current drives biology — is tractable, linear, comfortable. A bidirectional feedback loop is none of those things. If current speed predicts next-quarter species richness and species richness predicts next-quarter current speed, the system is coupled in the Granger sense: neither variable can be fully modeled without the other’s history. This is not correlation. It is temporal precedence — the closest SQL can come to causation without an experiment.

Scenario IV — The Topographic Amplifier

Does seafloor topography amplify or redirect cascading signals — do thermal anomalies propagate differently over ridges, trenches, and abyssal plains?

-- Topographic modulation of thermal cascade speed WITH profile_with_terrain AS ( SELECT p.profile_id, p.observation_ts, p.pressure_dbar, p.temperature_c, b.feature_type, b.depth_m AS seafloor_depth, DATE_TRUNC('month', p.observation_ts) AS obs_month FROM argo.float_profiles p JOIN gebco.bathymetry b ON ROUND(p.lat) = ROUND(b.lat) AND ROUND(p.lon) = ROUND(b.lon) WHERE p.quality_flag IN (1, 2) AND b.feature_type IN ('ridge', 'trench', 'abyssal_plain') ), topographic_amplifier AS ( SELECT feature_type, obs_month, AVG(CASE WHEN pressure_dbar < 100 THEN temperature_c END) AS surface_temp, AVG(CASE WHEN pressure_dbar > 500 THEN temperature_c END) AS deep_temp, AVG(CASE WHEN pressure_dbar < 100 THEN temperature_c END) - LAG(AVG(CASE WHEN pressure_dbar < 100 THEN temperature_c END), 1) OVER ( PARTITION BY feature_type ORDER BY obs_month ) AS surface_delta, AVG(CASE WHEN pressure_dbar > 500 THEN temperature_c END) - LAG(AVG(CASE WHEN pressure_dbar > 500 THEN temperature_c END), 6) OVER ( PARTITION BY feature_type ORDER BY obs_month ) AS deep_delta_lag6 FROM profile_with_terrain GROUP BY feature_type, obs_month ) SELECT feature_type, ROUND(CORR(surface_delta, deep_delta_lag6)::numeric, 4) AS cascade_correlation, ROUND((AVG(ABS(deep_delta_lag6)) / NULLIF(AVG(ABS(surface_delta)), 0))::numeric, 3) AS amplification_ratio, CASE WHEN AVG(ABS(deep_delta_lag6)) > AVG(ABS(surface_delta)) * 1.2 THEN 'amplifies' WHEN AVG(ABS(deep_delta_lag6)) < AVG(ABS(surface_delta)) * 0.5 THEN 'dampens' ELSE 'channels' END AS topographic_effect, COUNT(*) AS n_months FROM topographic_amplifier WHERE surface_delta IS NOT NULL AND deep_delta_lag6 IS NOT NULL GROUP BY feature_type ORDER BY amplification_ratio DESC;
Geometry shapes signal. A ridge forces upwelling, concentrating thermal energy into a narrower water column — the cascade amplifies. A trench channels flow along its axis, preserving signal strength over distance but redirecting its path. An abyssal plain, with its vast thermal inertia and minimal forcing, absorbs perturbations like a sponge. The same surface anomaly, propagated through the same six-month delay, arrives at the deep ocean with fundamentally different amplitude depending on what the seafloor looks like. The cascade is not just temporal. It is topographic.

Scenario V — The Ice-Temperature Cascade

As ice concentration changes, does the SST response cascade through adjacent ice-free regions — and how far does the thermal signal propagate from the ice edge?

-- Spatial radiation of thermal signal from ice edge WITH ice_edge AS ( SELECT grid_id, lat, lon, observation_date, sst_anomaly_c, ice_concentration_pct FROM noaa.sea_surface_temperature WHERE ice_concentration_pct BETWEEN 1 AND 50 ), open_water AS ( SELECT grid_id, lat, lon, observation_date, sst_anomaly_c FROM noaa.sea_surface_temperature WHERE ice_concentration_pct = 0 OR ice_concentration_pct IS NULL ), ice_radiation AS ( SELECT e.observation_date, SQRT(POWER(e.lat - w.lat, 2) + POWER(e.lon - w.lon, 2)) AS distance_deg, e.sst_anomaly_c AS edge_anomaly, w.sst_anomaly_c AS water_anomaly, CASE WHEN SQRT(POWER(e.lat - w.lat, 2) + POWER(e.lon - w.lon, 2)) < 2 THEN '0-2 deg' WHEN SQRT(POWER(e.lat - w.lat, 2) + POWER(e.lon - w.lon, 2)) < 5 THEN '2-5 deg' WHEN SQRT(POWER(e.lat - w.lat, 2) + POWER(e.lon - w.lon, 2)) < 10 THEN '5-10 deg' ELSE '10+ deg' END AS distance_band FROM ice_edge e JOIN open_water w ON e.observation_date = w.observation_date AND ABS(e.lat - w.lat) < 12 AND ABS(e.lon - w.lon) < 12 ) SELECT distance_band, ROUND(CORR(edge_anomaly, water_anomaly)::numeric, 4) AS cascade_correlation, ROUND(AVG(water_anomaly)::numeric, 3) AS mean_water_anomaly, ROUND(STDDEV(water_anomaly)::numeric, 3) AS anomaly_variability, ROUND((AVG(ABS(water_anomaly)) / NULLIF(AVG(ABS(edge_anomaly)), 0))::numeric, 3) AS signal_decay_ratio, COUNT(*) AS n_pairs FROM ice_radiation GROUP BY distance_band ORDER BY distance_band;
Ice retreat is not a local event. As the edge recedes, it exposes dark ocean to direct solar absorption — the albedo feedback — and that thermal pulse radiates outward. The correlation between ice-edge anomaly and open-water anomaly should decay with distance, but the rate of decay encodes the cascade’s spatial reach. If the 5–10 degree band still shows significant correlation, the cascade extends over 500 kilometers. The ice edge is not a boundary. It is a transmitter, broadcasting thermal information into the surrounding ocean at a rate determined by geometry, currents, and the season’s insolation.

Scenario VI — The Multi-Layer Cascade

Can we detect a signal that propagates through all layers of the ocean system — from atmosphere through surface through depth through biology — as a single coherent cascade?

-- The full cascade: atmosphere → surface → depth → biology WITH atmosphere AS ( SELECT ROUND(lat / 5) * 5 AS region_lat, ROUND(lon / 5) * 5 AS region_lon, DATE_TRUNC('month', observation_ts) AS obs_month, AVG(wind_speed_ms) AS mean_wind, AVG(air_temp_c) AS mean_air_temp FROM noaa.buoy_observations GROUP BY region_lat, region_lon, obs_month ), surface AS ( SELECT ROUND(lat / 5) * 5 AS region_lat, ROUND(lon / 5) * 5 AS region_lon, DATE_TRUNC('month', observation_date) AS obs_month, AVG(sst_anomaly_c) AS mean_sst_anomaly FROM noaa.sea_surface_temperature GROUP BY region_lat, region_lon, obs_month ), depth_layer AS ( SELECT ROUND(lat / 5) * 5 AS region_lat, ROUND(lon / 5) * 5 AS region_lon, DATE_TRUNC('month', observation_ts) AS obs_month, AVG(temperature_c) AS mean_deep_temp FROM argo.float_profiles WHERE pressure_dbar > 500 AND quality_flag IN (1, 2) GROUP BY region_lat, region_lon, obs_month ), biology AS ( SELECT ROUND(lat / 5) * 5 AS region_lat, ROUND(lon / 5) * 5 AS region_lon, DATE_TRUNC('month', observation_date) AS obs_month, COUNT(DISTINCT species_name) AS species_richness, SUM(abundance) AS total_abundance FROM obis.biodiversity_observations GROUP BY region_lat, region_lon, obs_month ), full_cascade AS ( SELECT a.region_lat, a.region_lon, a.obs_month, a.mean_air_temp, s.mean_sst_anomaly, d.mean_deep_temp, bio.species_richness, -- Lagged forward references through each layer LEAD(s.mean_sst_anomaly, 1) OVER w AS sst_next_1mo, LEAD(d.mean_deep_temp, 6) OVER w AS deep_next_6mo, LEAD(bio.species_richness, 9) OVER w AS bio_next_9mo FROM atmosphere a JOIN surface s ON a.region_lat = s.region_lat AND a.region_lon = s.region_lon AND a.obs_month = s.obs_month JOIN depth_layer d ON a.region_lat = d.region_lat AND a.region_lon = d.region_lon AND a.obs_month = d.obs_month JOIN biology bio ON a.region_lat = bio.region_lat AND a.region_lon = bio.region_lon AND a.obs_month = bio.obs_month WINDOW w AS ( PARTITION BY a.region_lat, a.region_lon ORDER BY a.obs_month ) ) SELECT -- Link 1: atmosphere → surface (1-month lag) ROUND(CORR(mean_air_temp, sst_next_1mo)::numeric, 4) AS atmo_to_surface, -- Link 2: atmosphere → depth (6-month lag) ROUND(CORR(mean_air_temp, deep_next_6mo)::numeric, 4) AS atmo_to_depth, -- Link 3: atmosphere → biology (9-month lag) ROUND(CORR(mean_air_temp, bio_next_9mo)::numeric, 4) AS atmo_to_biology, -- Cascade decay: does signal weaken at each step? CASE WHEN ABS(CORR(mean_air_temp, sst_next_1mo)) > ABS(CORR(mean_air_temp, deep_next_6mo)) AND ABS(CORR(mean_air_temp, deep_next_6mo)) > ABS(CORR(mean_air_temp, bio_next_9mo)) THEN 'monotonic decay — classical cascade' WHEN ABS(CORR(mean_air_temp, deep_next_6mo)) > ABS(CORR(mean_air_temp, sst_next_1mo)) THEN 'mid-layer amplification — emergent' ELSE 'non-monotonic — complex coupling' END AS cascade_topology, COUNT(*) AS n_observations FROM full_cascade WHERE sst_next_1mo IS NOT NULL AND deep_next_6mo IS NOT NULL AND bio_next_9mo IS NOT NULL;
This is the capstone. Four tables. Four layers of the Earth system. One cascade. An atmospheric perturbation — a shift in air temperature — should appear in SST within a month, in the mesopelagic within six months, and in biodiversity within nine. The correlation should decay at each transition if the cascade is classical. But if the mid-layer correlation is stronger than the surface coupling, the deep ocean is amplifying — Anderson’s emergence in action, detected with nothing more than window functions and a four-way join. No single table contains the cascade. No single discipline would think to look for it. The signal exists only in the coupling.

Coda

Six cascades. Four layers of the ocean system. Each query tracking how a perturbation at one scale propagates to another.

The thermal cascade shows depth-delay: surface warming takes six months to reach the mesopelagic, and the deep may amplify what the surface merely introduced. The wind-wave-temperature chain shows atmospheric coupling: a mediation structure unfolding over hours, where the intermediate link (waves) carries more predictive information than the direct path. The current-biology feedback shows bidirectional causality: the rarest and most dangerous structure in complex systems, where cause and effect reverse depending on which quarter you examine.

The topographic amplifier shows that geometry shapes propagation: the same signal, over the same delay, arrives with different amplitude depending on whether the seafloor is a ridge, a trench, or a plain. The ice cascade shows spatial radiation: the ice edge broadcasting thermal information outward, with signal strength decaying as a function of distance in a pattern that encodes the coupling strength of the polar ocean. And the multi-layer cascade shows the full chain — atmosphere to surface to depth to biology — a single coherent signal threading through four tables, four disciplines, four timescales.

Single-scale analysis sees none of this. A surface oceanographer sees warming. A deep-ocean physicist sees circulation change. A marine biologist sees biodiversity shift. An atmospheric scientist sees wind patterns. Each sees their slice. None sees the cascade that connects the slices — because the cascade is not in any slice. It is in the coupling between them.

Anderson was right. More is different. The cascade is not a sum of its layers. It is an emergent property of their interaction — detectable only by the observer who watches multiple layers simultaneously with calibrated delay.

We demonstrated this on seawater because seawater is patient, layered, and public. Every cascade above could be re-pointed — at supply chains, at neural circuits, at institutional feedback loops, at market contagion, at epidemic propagation — without changing a single structural element. The SQL would change. The tables would change. The cascade architecture would not.

The framework was never designed for the ocean alone.
